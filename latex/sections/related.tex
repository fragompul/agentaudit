\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Statistics for LLM evaluation.} \citet{miller2024error} gives standard errors, clustered errors, paired differences and power formulae for single-run question-answering evals; \citet{bowyer2025clt} show that central-limit intervals fail for small benchmarks and recommend Bayesian or exact alternatives; \citet{madaan2024variance} quantify seed variance for model training and evaluation; \citet{biderman2024lessons} document reproducibility pitfalls. Earlier work in NLP and RL made the same case for significance testing, power and robust aggregation \citep{dror2018hitchhiker,card2020power,agarwal2021precipice,bouthillier2021variance}. We build on this literature but target agents: several attempts per task, long-horizon outcomes verified by state, and the question of how to spend a budget.

\paragraph{Agent evaluation and reliability.} Tau-bench introduced pass$^k$ to measure consistency across trials \citep{yao2024taubench}; \citet{kapoor2024matter} argued that agent benchmarks must account for cost and reproducibility; HAL standardised large-scale agent evaluation \citep{kapoor2026hal}. \citet{huang2026leaderboard} propose an estimand-aware pairwise procedure for agent leaderboards, and find unresolved close rank differences on SWE-bench and tau2-bench; \citet{neuhof2026ranking} build rank intervals for MMLU from pairwise tests and analyse the sources of ranking uncertainty across subjects. Our contributions are complementary: a crossed variance decomposition over agents, tasks and runs, closed-form allocation of a budget between tasks and repeats, validation against synthetic ground truth, an offline benchmark with controlled stochasticity, and an all-pairs re-analysis with multiplicity control.

\paragraph{Anytime-valid inference.} Confidence sequences \citep{howard2021cs} and betting-based constructions for bounded means \citep{waudbysmith2024betting} permit continuous monitoring. Recent work applies them to LLM evaluation and online ranking \citep{hsu2026sequential,gu2026serpant}. We use the betting confidence sequence for paired per-task differences and measure, in simulation, what it costs and what it buys relative to fixed-sample tests and to naive peeking.
