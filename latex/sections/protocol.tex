\section{Experimental Protocol}\label{sec:protocol}
All simulation experiments were specified before they were run (\texttt{docs/experiments.md}: question, design, result that would support the claim, result that would contradict it); unfavourable outcomes are reported below. Every random stream is seeded, the scripts are in \texttt{scripts/}, and every number in this paper is produced from \texttt{results/} by \texttt{scripts/make\_paper\_assets.py}.

\paragraph{Simulated agents.} Synthetic agents follow a logit-normal item-response model, $p_{mt}=\sigma(\theta_m-b_t+g_{mt})$ with $b_t\sim N(0,\sigma_b^2)$ and agent-by-task interaction $g_{mt}\sim N(0,\sigma_g^2)$, and $Y_{mtr}\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(p_{mt})$ independently across runs. Population scores, pass$^k$ and differences are computed by Gauss--Hermite quadrature, so coverage, error rates and power are measured against exact truth. The model is a convenient generator, not a claim about real agents; the robustness experiments vary its parameters.

\paragraph{Experiments.} \textbf{S0} (RQ1, RQ2, RQ4, ranking calibration): the original coverage, type-I error, fixed-budget design, sequential and ranking simulations, \simReps{} replications per coverage cell. \textbf{E1} (RQ1): type-I error of the paired tests when $d_t$ is skewed with mean zero (weak null), $T\in\{20,50,100,300\}$, $R\in\{1,5\}$, \asymReps{} replications. \textbf{E2} (RQ2): tasks versus repeats over task heterogeneity $\times$ interaction, \allocReps{} replications. \textbf{E3} (RQ1): coverage across prevalence, heterogeneity, $T$ and $R$, \covrobReps{} replications. \textbf{E5} (RQ4): sequential stopping with margins $\{0,0.03,0.06,0.10\}$ and minimum task counts, \seqmReps{} replications. \textbf{E6} (RQ3): pairwise distinguishability and Holm family-wise error on a six-agent leaderboard with an exactly tied pair, \rankpwReps{} replications. (The fixed-budget comparison was not repeated on the local-model data; see Section~\ref{sec:ablation}. S0 designates the simulations of the first version of this study; they were re-run unchanged and reproduce the stored results exactly.) Monte Carlo standard errors are about 0.6 points for a rejection rate near 5\% with 1500 replications and 0.7 points for a coverage near 95\% with 1000.

\paragraph{SWE-bench Verified (RQ3).} Per-instance resolved lists of every submission in the public \texttt{swe-bench/experiments} repository that declares at least 50 resolved instances, \sweNAgents{} entries on \sweNTasks{} instances, analysed with exact McNemar tests and Holm/Benjamini--Hochberg adjustment (one binary outcome per task and entry). The public files do not state evaluation coverage or the number of attempts, so we audit them: an entry's coverage is \emph{verified} when the union of its public log folders and its \texttt{no\_generation} instances is exactly the 500 instances (\sweNCovered{} entries; \sweNUnverif{} have no public logs and \sweNIncomplete{} are incomplete), and the \texttt{attempts} field of its metadata gives one attempt for \sweNSingle{} entries, several for \sweNMulti{} (best-of-$n$ or multi-sample systems) and is missing for \sweNAttUnknown. The headline analysis keeps all entries; sensitivity analyses keep coverage-verified entries, single-attempt entries, and both.

\paragraph{$\tau$-bench (RQ3).} Historical trajectories of gpt-4o and Claude 3.5 Sonnet (new, 2024-10-22 release), the first four trials per task, released by the authors under the MIT license. Only derived aggregates are stored in this repository.

\paragraph{Local models (RQ5).} \REALPROTOCOL{} The main run did not enable the fault injector (fault rate 0), so reliability under tool failure is not evaluated here.
